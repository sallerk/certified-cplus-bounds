The constant

Carneiro, Milinovich and Soundararajan (arXiv:1708.04122, Extremal Problem 2) define, for a parameter A,

C₊(A) = sup [ F(0) − A ∫|t|>1 (F̂(t))₊ dt ] / ‖F‖₁

the supremum over even, continuous, real F in L¹(ℝ). Larger is better: a bigger C₊ gives a smaller constant in a bound on gaps between primes. Chirre and Quesada-Herrera (arXiv:2012.07781) tabulate lower bounds at 68 values of A, and use A = 28 for primes represented by a binary quadratic form.

Any admissible F gives a valid lower bound. So the whole game is finding a good test function — and then proving what it is worth.

What changed

The published records come from hand-tuned families of three or four parameters. Replacing that search with two optimizers over 100–200 coefficients beats them at every value of A:

  • a linear program over a cosine series for F̂, band-limited to [−B,B];
  • a semidefinite program over F(x) = P(x)e−πx², following Chirre–Pereira–de Laat, where sign conditions become sum-of-squares constraints.

Neither dominates. The linear program wins for 1.5 ≤ A ≤ 9; the semidefinite program wins at A = 1 and for A ≥ 9.5. Running both and taking the better one is what gives 68 out of 68.

What “certified” means here

Ordinary floating-point arithmetic returns a number with no guarantee. Every quantity below is instead computed in ball arithmetic — a value plus a radius, with the true result provably inside — using ARB, the same library the published record was certified with.

Because the claim is a lower bound on a fraction, the errors must be forced in a specific direction: an upper bound on the denominator ‖F‖₁, and an upper bound on the subtracted penalty. Three details carry the proof:

  • Coefficients are fixed as exact rationals and projected so F̂(B) = 0 holds exactly. Without this ‖F‖₁ is infinite and nothing is proved.
  • ∫sinc = Si/π in closed form, so no quadrature error exists anywhere. The only work is deciding signs, and intervals that cannot be decided are charged at their maximum — so no claim about having found every root is needed.
  • The tail beyond the computed range is proved to decay like 1/x³ with an explicit constant, not observed to be small.

The certificate was tested in both directions: it accepts the true bound and refuses a target below the true supremum, reporting exactly where it fails.

How much room is left

A lower bound alone says nothing about how close to optimal it is. Optimizing the dual side numerically — the same duality CMS use analytically — gives certified upper bounds:

1.0951787824934853 ≤ C₊(28) ≤ 1.1079

a bracket of 1.16%, against roughly 10.6% from the previously published upper bound. So the bulk of the apparent gap was slack in the upper bound, not room in the lower one. At A = 2 the bracket closes to under 1%.

The duality gap is not proven, so the residual cannot be attributed to either side — the true value is known to lie in the interval, not where in it.

Consequence

2 / C₊(28) ≤ 1.8261858538 · h(−D)

improving the constant in Chirre–Quesada-Herrera's Corollary 5, on gaps between primes represented by a binary quadratic form, from their printed 1.837 h(−D).

What this is not

  • Not new mathematics. The extremal problem is CMS's, the constants and the application are Chirre–Quesada-Herrera's, and the semidefinite formulation is Chirre–Pereira–de Laat's. New here are the test functions and their certificates.
  • The SDP does not exactly reproduce published SDP values — it lands 4–5×10−4 low at matched degree, and the discrepancy is unexplained. This does not affect the bounds: the semidefinite program is only a generator of candidate functions, and each candidate is certified by evaluating it rigorously.
  • A = 1 is not a record. CMS prove C₊(1) = 2 exactly; the certificate reaches 99.94% of it.
  • The record check is thorough but not exhaustive. Three citation indexes and author sweeps found no improvement since 2022, but MathSciNet, zbMATH and Google Scholar were unreachable, and the indexes provably undercount.
  • Certification rests on ARB's correctness — unavoidable, and shared with the published record it improves.
